# Step 2: Concept

## Rule

A new idea is an old way of thinking carried into today's conditions. Not the old look:
the old *question*. Earlier makers each answered a question with the means of their time.
Ask what that question becomes now, notice what has become free and what has become scarce,
and build the piece on the scarce thing.

Copying an earlier device as it was is pastiche, and fails this step. Generating ideas from
effects ("glitchy neon tunnel") is not a concept at all.

## Method

1. **Purpose as a question.** From the spec, write what the piece must do as a question:
   "How does a bank feel precise without feeling cold?" "How does a techno room stay locked in
   for three hours?"
2. **Pick three ways of thinking** from the [Ideas](../idea/ideas.md) index, from three
   different families. Read each page. Pick by the question it answered, not by its look.
3. **Transpose each one.** Fill this for each idea, using [Now](../idea/now.md):

```text
idea:          (page id)
then asked:    the question it answered, in its own time
now asks:      the same question under today's conditions
became free:   what was hard then and is cheap or automatic now
became scarce: what is now rare, valuable or missing
opening:       what the piece does with the scarce thing (from the idea's Openings, or your own)
```

4. **Write each as a concept sentence:** "<subject> <verb> like <material or system>, in
   <space>". It names what moves, how, and why that motion means the purpose.
5. **Score** each with the table below. Keep the highest; ties go to the simplest.
6. **Write the two losers down** with one line on why they lost. Borrow from them later only
   for secondary motion.

## Scoring

| Question | 0 | 1 | 2 |
|---|---|---|---|
| Does the motion itself carry the purpose? | decoration | loosely | the verb is the message |
| Does it only make sense now? | a template could make it | a known move in a new context | it answers today's conditions |
| Can it be read in a 1-second glance? | no | with help | yes |
| Is it buildable in the medium within the brief? | no | with risk | yes |
| Does it fit the first mood word? | against | neutral | embodies it |

A concept that scores 0 on "only makes sense now" cannot win, whatever its total.

## Example

Brief: a fintech brand must feel precise and trustworthy; 5 s logo sting.

```text
idea:          identity-as-system
then asked:    how can a brand stay recognizable while its picture changes every time?
now asks:      every logo can be animated smoothly by a template; what makes motion mean the brand?
became free:   smooth, polished logo animation
became scarce: motion that states the company's own principle
opening:       the mark is the end state of a rule (order emerging), not a drawing that moves
```

Concept: "Scattered points lock into a lattice, and the lattice is the mark." Score 9.
Losers: "ink settles into the mark" (see-the-sound family; fluid reads calm, not precise, 6) and
"the mark is typed by a cursor" (a template move, 0 on now, out).

## Don't

- Don't pick ideas by their look. Two ideas from the same family give one concept twice.
- Don't skip the transposition table. Its "became scarce" line is where the idea comes from.
- Don't describe the concept with mood words; those are in the spec already.
