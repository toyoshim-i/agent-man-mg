# Math is the image

## Question

What if a formula, made visible, is the picture: no assets, no models, only mathematics
running in real time?

## Then

Programmers in the home-computer era competed to make the most impressive real-time
audiovisual films on fixed hardware. Size categories followed: complete films with music
in 64 kilobytes, then 4 kilobytes, then less. Nothing could be stored, so everything was
generated: textures from noise, worlds from distance functions, music from synthesis. When
graphics hardware became programmable, a whole art form moved into fragment shaders, and a
website let anyone publish a shader as a living image with its source beside it. Beauty
became inseparable from the elegance of the formula, and viewers who could read code
admired both at once.

## On screen

- Infinite detail from small rules: fractals, repetition, domain warping, signed distance fields.
- Light as the main material: soft shadows, ambient occlusion, fog, glow.
- Camera moves that reveal scale: a fly-through that shows the space is endless.
- A single mathematical idea per piece, carried to its limit.

Anchors: farbrausch, *fr-08: .the .product* (2000, 64 KB). Shadertoy (2013).

## Now

Real-time GPU is in every browser and phone, and generated images can imitate the look of
raymarched worlds without any math. So the look of shader art no longer proves anything. What
stays scarce: the idea in the formula, made legible; work that is live (it responds, it
never repeats); the honesty of an image that is exactly its code.

## Openings

- Show the formula: the piece reveals its own rule (a parameter changing, the structure being
  built) so the viewer sees the math, not just its result.
- Live and endless: a shader that runs as long as the venue is open and never repeats a frame,
  driven by the room (sound, time of day, people).
- One idea, many briefs: a brand whose identity is one function, rendered differently everywhere.
