# See the sound

## Question

How can image and sound be one score, so that seeing and hearing are the same experience?

## Then

Early twentieth-century painters wanted painting to work like music: no subject, only form,
color and rhythm. Film added time, and abstraction became playable. Animators drew geometric
forms frame by frame to records. One drew sound directly onto the film's optical soundtrack,
so the marks you saw and the tones you heard were the same marks. Later, artists built
analog computers to generate motion: points moving at speeds in whole-number ratios, which
drift into disorder and lock back into figures, the way dissonance resolves into harmony.
It was a reply to cinema as storytelling: an image could be music instead of illustrating a plot.

## On screen

- No objects, only forms. Each voice in the music owns a shape family.
- Pitch maps to height, loudness to size or brightness, timbre to edge (soft or hard).
- Harmonic motion: N points rotating at speeds 1, 2, 3 ... times a base speed. Most of the
  time they scatter; at regular moments they align into a figure. Alignment is the cadence.
- Sync on phrases and gestures, not on every note: a sweep for a run, a hold for a held chord.

Anchors: Oskar Fischinger, *An Optical Poem* (1938). Norman McLaren, *Synchromy* (1971).
John Whitney, *Catalog* (1961) and *Arabesque* (1975).

## Now

Sync became free. Every VJ application and editing template makes things pulse to audio.
So pulsing reads as a default, not as music made visible. Scarce: a mapping chosen for
meaning; restraint; counterpoint, where the image does what the music does not. Harmonic
convergence is cheap to compute and rarely used with intent.

## Openings

- One element listens; everything else holds still. The listening becomes visible.
- Counterpoint: the image keeps the pulse while the music breaks down, then lets go on the drop.
- Converge on the cadence: points in harmonic ratios align into the logo exactly at the phrase end.
- Teach the mapping in ten seconds, then break it once. The break is felt because it was learned.
