# Instrument as actor

## Question

How can a viewer see the structure of a piece of music: which parts play, when they enter,
how they repeat?

## Then

Electronic dance music is built from loops: a drum pattern, a bass line, a riff, each
entering and leaving. Music video directors in the 1990s and 2000s noticed that this
structure is invisible to most listeners. They cast each loop as something on screen: a
group of dancers per instrument, each repeating its own move whenever its part plays; or a
train ride where every passing object (a pole, a bridge, a factory) lands on a specific
drum or synth hit, so the landscape becomes the score. It answered the "performance video"
convention, where the band plays and nothing explains the song.

## On screen

- A cast: one figure, object or place per layer of the arrangement (kick, bass, hat, lead, vocal).
- Each cast member has one move and repeats it only while its layer plays.
- Entrances and exits of layers are entrances and exits of cast members.
- Repetition is exact, so the eye learns the mapping within two bars.
- The viewer's pleasure is recognition: "that is the bass".

Anchors: Daft Punk, "Around the World" (Michel Gondry, 1997). The Chemical Brothers,
"Star Guitar" (Michel Gondry, 2002).

## Now

Stems are easy to get and audio analysis can separate layers, so mapping can be automated.
What automation does not give: a cast that has character, and a choice of world in which
those characters make sense together. Short vertical formats leave room for only two or
three cast members, which forces the choice.

## Openings

- A typographic cast: each layer is a word or a glyph, and the lyric is assembled by the arrangement.
- An interface cast: each layer is a UI element (a cursor, a notification, a progress bar).
  A portrait of daily screens, played by the song.
- Live: in a VJ set, give each deck or channel a cast member, so the DJ's mix is visible as
  who is on stage.
