# World across media

## Question

How can one world live across songs, videos, stories and characters, so that each work is an
entrance to the same place?

## Then

A virtual band existed only as drawn characters, with music videos, interviews and websites
all in their world. In Japan, a songwriter released a series of songs with videos that told
one story about a group of characters; the story expanded into novels, manga and an anime,
and fans pieced it together across all of them. A music duo built songs from short stories
written on a fiction site, so each song was the second medium of a story that already
existed. Japan's long tradition of the media mix (manga, anime, games, music for one world)
made this natural.

## On screen

- Recurring symbols, colors and silhouettes that identify the world in any medium.
- Each piece contains details that belong to the larger story (an object, a date, a place).
- Pieces can be watched alone; together they reward attention.
- Visual language held across the series, while each piece varies one thing.

Anchors: Gorillaz (from 2000). Jin's Kagerou Project (from 2011). YOASOBI's songs based on
short stories (from 2019).

## Now

Franchises are built across media by default, and generated content can fill a world with
endless material. Volume is free. Scarce: coherence; details that reward attention; a world
rather than a stream of content.

## Openings

- One artifact per piece: every short video carries one object from the world; across the
  series they assemble into the full picture.
- A brand as a place: its pieces are views from different locations in one world, with a map.
- A VJ set as a journey through one world, where each scene is a district.
