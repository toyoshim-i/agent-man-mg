# Work as node

## Question

What if a work is not finished when it is released, but made to be continued by others?

## Then

On a Japanese video site around voice synthesis software, one song set off a chain: someone
drew an illustration for it, another made a video with that illustration, singers posted
covers, dancers posted choreography, others remixed the track or made 3D dance videos. Credits
read like a network. Derivative creation (*niji sousaku*) was the culture, not an exception.
An advertisement for a web browser told this story in a music video: the song's creators and
fans connected by lines across the world. A pop group released its motion capture data and
stems so that anyone could build on them.

## On screen

- Visible connections: lines, threads, handoffs between contributors.
- Open slots: an instrumental section, a blank space, an empty layer, an unfinished pose
  that invites completion.
- Many versions shown side by side as one work.
- Credits and sources as part of the design, not hidden at the end.

Anchors: Niconico's Vocaloid culture from 2007. livetune, "Tell Your World" (2012).
Perfume Global Site Project (2012, open motion data).

## Now

Remix is built into platforms (sounds, duets, stitches, templates), and generated content
remixes everything without asking. Participation is frictionless and anonymous. Scarce:
a designed invitation (a specific gap people want to fill); visible lineage and credit; the
sense of a community rather than a feed.

## Openings

- Ship the parts: release the piece with its template, palette, loops or shader parameters,
  and design it so variations look good.
- A VJ loop with an empty layer: the set is completed by guest visuals or audience input.
- Call and response: the piece ends on a question (an unfinished motion, a blank) addressed
  to the next maker.
