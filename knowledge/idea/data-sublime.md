# Data sublime

## Question

How can something too large to grasp (a dataset, the universe, a network) be felt rather
than explained?

## Then

The sublime is the old idea that vastness overwhelms the senses and produces awe. Artists
working with data in the 2000s made it physical: streams of numbers, bars and pixels rushing
across huge screens in pure black and white, synchronized to sharp clicks and sine tones, with
scale shifting from a single bit to the whole universe. Precision was part of the effect: every
pixel exact, every sound aligned. Later, machine learning let artists turn huge image
collections into flowing, liquid "data paintings" on architectural LED walls.

## On screen

- Extreme density: thousands of elements, rendered sharp, never blurred.
- Extreme contrast: black and white, or one color; light as information.
- Scale jumps: from a single row of data to an overview where it becomes texture, and back.
- Hard sync between image and sound: every flicker has a click.
- Speed beyond reading: the viewer stops trying to read and starts to feel.

Anchors: Ryoji Ikeda, *datamatics* (from 2006). Refik Anadol's machine-learning data
paintings (2010s onward).

## Now

Data visualization is everywhere, and flowing generated "data paintings" have become lobby
decoration. Overwhelming spectacle is cheap. Scarce: data with stakes (where the numbers
matter to the viewer); specificity; the single datum inside the mass.

## Openings

- From sublime to personal: zoom from millions of data points to one that belongs to the viewer,
  the place, or the moment, and hold there.
- Live data of the room: the venue's sound, the crowd's phones, the time, the weather, rendered
  at full density, so the sublime is about here and now.
- Slowness as contrast: one datum at a time, very slowly, in a feed of overwhelming speed.
