# The screen dissolves

## Question

What if the image leaves the rectangle and becomes space, surface or architecture?

## Then

In the 1960s, artists surrounded audiences with multiple projections and light, calling it
expanded cinema. Later, projection mapping fitted video exactly onto buildings and objects:
a facade could crack, breathe or fall apart. Electronic musicians built stage sets that were
themselves screens: a performer inside a pyramid of light; a stack of white cubes onto
which a whole world of machinery was projected so that the structure seemed to transform.
Immersive rooms put the audience inside the image. Huge curved LED billboards painted a
3D illusion that worked from one street corner.

## On screen

- Content made for the geometry: edges, corners and depth of the real surface are the composition.
- Illusions that confirm and then break the surface: the wall seems to open, then closes.
- The audience's position matters: anamorphic images resolve from one viewpoint only.
- The whole field of view, so there is no "outside the frame".

Anchors: Daft Punk's pyramid stage (2006 to 2007). Amon Tobin, *ISAM* live (2011).
teamLab's immersive rooms. The 3D cat billboard in Shinjuku (2021).

## Now

LED surfaces are everywhere and immersive rooms are a commercial format; anamorphic billboards
are mostly experienced as phone videos of them. Scarce: site specificity; the experience that
does not survive being recorded; content that is about the place it is on.

## Openings

- Made for the room, not the recording: effects that only work in peripheral vision, or with
  haze, or at a scale the phone cannot hold.
- The architecture's own story: mapping that reveals the history or structure of the building.
- For VJ: treat the LED rig's shape as the composition grid, so the visuals could not be
  played on any other stage.
