# Constraint as style

## Question

How does a limitation become a signature, instead of a weakness to hide?

## Then

Again and again, a style was born from what could not be afforded. Television animation in
Japan could not pay for full animation, so it drew fewer frames, held images, moved only a
mouth or a strand of hair, and made the holds dramatic: limited animation became a grammar
of its own. Early web animation had tiny files and vector tools, so it became flat color,
thick outlines and looping symbols, and a generation of makers grew up in it. Programmers
made real-time films that had to fit in 4 or 64 kilobytes, so everything was generated by
formula. Song videos made with one illustration made the lyrics carry the motion. In each
case the makers stopped apologizing for the limit and pushed it to the extreme.

## On screen

- One limit, stated and obeyed completely: two colors, 12 drawings per second, one shape,
  one font, one shot, 10 seconds, 4 KB.
- The limit is visible. A viewer can name it in a few seconds.
- Craft goes into what remains: if only two colors, the shapes are exquisite; if few frames,
  the poses are strong.

## Now

The material limits are gone: any tool can render anything, and generated imagery removes
even the limit of skill. So limits now have to be chosen, and a chosen limit reads as a
voice. Scarce: commitment; a recognizable rule that holds across a series.

## Openings

- Pick the limit from the brief's world: a bank sting made only of the characters on a receipt
  printer; a techno loop with one line and nothing else.
- Make the limit the subject: a counter shows how much of the budget (frames, colors, bytes)
  has been spent.
- A series rule: every piece from this client uses the same three constraints, so the
  constraints become the brand.
