# Character as vessel

## Question

How can a nearly blank character carry the stories of thousands of different makers?

## Then

A voice synthesis product was sold with a character on the box: a name, an age, a height, a
signature hair color and costume, and almost no story. Because the canon was nearly empty,
everyone who used the voice wrote her a different personality, song, and world. Illustrators
drew her in every style; a free 3D tool let anyone make her dance; she appeared in concerts as
a projected figure in front of a live band. She became less a character than a shared medium.
Virtual streamers later took the idea further: a designed body worn by a real person.

## On screen

- A silhouette and a color that survive any drawing style: recognizable at thumbnail size
  in any rendering.
- The character hosts the song's world rather than telling her own story.
- Different makers' versions coexist; the variation is part of the identity.
- Presence without a body: projection, hologram-like screens, figures in light.

Anchors: Hatsune Miku (Crypton Future Media, 2007). MikuMikuDance (2008).

## Now

Avatars, virtual streamers and generated characters are everywhere; a character design is
free. Scarce: a character that a community owns and extends; a design that knows exactly
which parts are fixed and which are left open.

## Openings

- A mascot with a minimal canon: define silhouette, color and one motion trait only, and
  invite the audience to fill the rest.
- An abstract character: a shape with a color and a motion personality (how it waits, how it
  reacts), no face. The motion is the character.
- A character whose design is a rule, so every appearance is generated but recognizable.
