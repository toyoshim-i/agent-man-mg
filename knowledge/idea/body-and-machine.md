# Body and machine

## Question

What happens when a human body and a machine perform together: who leads, who follows,
and what is visible in the gap?

## Then

Choreographers worked with motion capture to turn dancers' movement into drawn figures,
detached from the body. Pop performance in Japan paired highly precise human choreography
with engineers: projection mapped onto moving costumes, light that followed bodies exactly,
drones and robotic arms moving with the dancers, augmented reality layered over live
broadcasts. The group also released its motion capture data and music openly, inviting
fans to make their own works from the dancers' movement. The marvel was precision on both
sides: humans as exact as machines, machines as graceful as humans.

## On screen

- Graphics attached to tracked bodies: lines between joints, trails from hands, particles
  shed by movement.
- Projection or light that fits a moving body so precisely that the two fuse.
- Mirrors and doubles: the body and its abstracted copy moving together, then diverging.
- The friction: latency, mistracking, a machine that cannot quite follow.

Anchors: Perfume with Rhizomatiks (Daito Manabe) and choreographer MIKIKO, 2010s live shows.

## Now

Body tracking runs on every phone (AR filters, pose estimation), and generated video makes
dancing figures from nothing. Tracked graphics are a filter. Scarce: real human virtuosity,
synchronized live; the visible relationship between a specific body and the machine.

## Openings

- The machine learns the body's imperfection: the graphic copies the dancer's small errors,
  and the error becomes the style.
- Deliberate lag: the visual double follows a beat late, so the body leads time itself.
- A performer plays the visuals: their movement is the controller of a VJ set, visible as such.
