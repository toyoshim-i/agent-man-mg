# Transition as meaning

## Question

What if the cut between two images says something that neither image says alone?

## Then

A famous film cut from a bone thrown into the air to a spacecraft of the same shape: millions
of years of tool-making in one cut. A Japanese animation director built films out of
transitions that slipped between reality, film, memory and dream without warning: a
character opens a door in one world and steps into another, a stage set becomes a real
street. The viewer realizes only afterwards that the boundary was crossed. The transition
was the story's argument: these two things are the same, or one becomes the other.

## On screen

- A shape, motion or position that continues across the cut while everything else changes.
- The relation stated by the match: same shape (identity), same motion (cause), same place
  (time passing).
- Seamless slips that the viewer notices late, and rereads.
- Few transitions, each meaningful, rather than a transition between every shot.

Anchors: *2001: A Space Odyssey* (1968), the bone-to-satellite cut. Satoshi Kon,
*Millennium Actress* (2001) and *Paprika* (2006).

## Now

Transition packs (zooms, whips, glitches) are one tap in every editor, and generated video can
morph anything into anything. Transitions are free and meaningless by default. Scarce: a
transition that states a relationship; restraint.

## Openings

- The transition is the message: a brand piece made of one transition between "before" and
  "after", carried by one shape.
- Morph with an argument: only morph between things that are claimed to be the same.
- The late realization: a loop whose transition is understood only on the second view.
