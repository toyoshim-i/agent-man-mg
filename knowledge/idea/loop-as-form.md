# Loop as form

## Question

What if a piece has no beginning and no end, and is meant to be watched again and again?

## Then

Before cinema, spinning devices showed short cycles of drawings: a horse running forever.
Cinema chose stories with endings. The loop came back with the animated GIF, a format that
plays forever in a web page, and with a short-video app that limited clips to six seconds and
replayed them automatically: makers learned to hide the seam so a clip could be watched
twenty times. Cinemagraphs froze a photograph except for one moving part. VJs built whole
sets from loops locked to the beat, and social feeds filled with perfectly looping 3D
animations that are pleasant precisely because they never end.

## On screen

- No seam: the last frame flows into the first.
- A cycle with an internal story: tension and release inside the loop.
- One moving part in a still world (cinemagraph), or an infinite zoom or scroll that is
  self-similar.
- Rewards on repeat: a detail noticed only on the third viewing.

Anchors: zoetrope and phenakistiscope (19th century). Vine (2013 to 2017). GIF art and
cinemagraphs (2010s).

## Now

Feeds autoplay and replay everything; loops are the default unit, and perfect seamless loops
are a technique anyone can learn. Scarce: a loop that changes meaning on each repeat; a loop
whose seam is the point; stillness that makes the one moving thing matter.

## Openings

- The twist at the seam: the loop point reveals that the end is the beginning, and the
  meaning flips on second viewing.
- A loop that slowly progresses: each cycle differs slightly (a counter, a growing element),
  so the loop is also a timeline.
- For VJ: a loop that knows the phrase, so its internal event lands on the music's change.
