# The screen decides

## Question

How does the place where a work is watched shape what the work is?

## Then

Each viewing surface produced its own forms. Television's small, low-resolution screen
favored close-ups, big type and safe margins. A Japanese video site laid viewers' comments
over the picture as scrolling text, so videos were made with room for comments and with
moments designed to make the comments burst (a "curtain" of text becomes part of the image).
A clothing brand made a clock for blogs: a widget showing the time with looping dance clips
and music, so the work lived inside other people's pages and was watched again whenever
someone checked the time. Phones turned the frame vertical, with platform buttons covering
the edges and sound often off.

## On screen

- Composition built around the surface's furniture: UI overlays, comment lanes, captions.
- Timing built around the surface's behavior: the scroll, the autoplay, the replay, the glance.
- The surface's own signals used as content: time of day, scroll position, the comment flood.

Anchors: Niconico's comment overlay. *Uniqlock* (Yugo Nakamura and team, 2007).

## Now

One piece is cut to many surfaces at once (9:16, 1:1, 16:9, LED wall), often automatically.
Platform-native forms (stories, duets, stitches) come as templates. Scarce: work that
knows exactly where it is and could not live anywhere else.

## Openings

- The platform's interface as material: the progress bar, the caption, the like button, the
  scroll gesture appear in the composition as actors.
- A piece that knows the clock and the place (morning in this city, the last train time), and
  changes with them.
- Design for the glance: the piece works as a still at 0.5 s and as a story at 15 s.
